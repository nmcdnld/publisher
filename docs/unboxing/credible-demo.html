Credible demo workspace: a front door for Publisher

Before getting access to the actual Credible web app, I wanted to build out a standalone prototype to explore Malloy-based content consumption and discoverability UX: a front door to Publisher where the workspace's packages fill its library with content from day one, AI drafts insights from it, and anything worth keeping saves back into the package it came from.

Noah Mac · product + design engineering update · Sept 28 - Oct 1, 2026
TL;DR
  • The Library starts full of content. Every dashboard, notebook, data app, and model contained by the packages installed on the workspace is shown from a unified Library view, next to what people have saved and posted outside the package.
  • A Publish button in the Console. Any query result or dashboard can be handed over to the Credible workspace for review, iteration, or just for a more consumption-oriented experience when sharing data.
  • A promote button in the workspace. Going the other way, Save keeps a chat response, query result, or AI generated insight in your personal library, or can write it into its package as saved content for everyone in the workspace to access.
  • A new "insight" content primitive. One app.json manifest captures a finding described in text, the queries behind it, the data app it uses, and a snapshot of the rows. It lives in the package, versioned with the model, and every query must compile against its package before it can be saved.
  • Autonomous AI insights, with a person in the loop. The Studio drafts grounded insights on a schedule, and a configurable gate allows the workspace admin to approve or reject each one before it reaches the team's For you pages. What people read, save, dismiss, and follow steers what the Studio looks for next. Agents referenced in the comment threads can make iterative improvements, fix bugs, and make note of follow-up topics to investigate. Over time, the generated content goes from reporting on the facts contained by the model to proposing new additions to the model.
  • A workspace that feels like home. Usually a premium feature, each workspace has its own whitelabeling customization options including its name, logo, and theme. Every style token, including the chart palette, is re-derived for light and dark, and embedded Publisher content respects the same tokens.

01The demo workspace

A separate app within the Publisher repo, built first for the people who read and act on data rather than the people who model it. It builds on top of the Publisher REST API.

Part 1 ended with two packages full of content and an explorer that assumes its user already knows Malloy or software engineering concepts. This prototype is my guess at where everyone else arrives, and how they discover what's already there: a consumption-oriented workspace that knows what content its packages serve, drafts insights from them, and saves anything worth keeping back into the package it came from, so the model stays the source of truth.

Surface Route What it's for
For You / The briefing, AI-drafted insight cards, what's trending, and where you left off
Library /library Personal and workspace scopes; everything packages serve plus everything your team saved to the workspace
Chats /chat/:id Threads with the AI analyst; every answer is grounded and can be saved as an insight
Insight /insight/:id One finding: a description, query, results, comments, and related content
Package pages /packages/:pkg/{dashboards,models,notebooks,apps}/… Publisher content rendered inside the shell, with details and related content

Stack. The prototype lives in packages/credible-demo and runs on :5180 over Publisher on :4000. It uses React 19, Vite, Tailwind and shadcn/Radix for the shell, and the Publisher SDK for embedded dashboards, models, notebooks and data apps.

The AI analyst runs as a Cloudflare Worker at /api/analyst/*, served through Vite in development. It has two stages. An explorer subagent uses four tools (list_sources, describe_source, run_query, compute_metric) and returns findings. A presenter subagent turns those findings into a report spec.

02The workspace Library, full on day one

A Library that starts empty is slower to fill. This one is a unified view of everything the workspace's packages already publish, next to what people have saved and posted more casually.

Related content derived from the source code

Every page's structured details panel links its related content. The links are read from what the files declare (imports, sources, tile queries, model paths in a data app's page), never guessed from names or tags. On an insight, that means Depends on storefront.malloy, Also reads order_items (five other analyses), and Referenced in the chat thread that produced it. This builds a content graph that the composer and For you page's autonomous recommendation loop can leverage.

04For You, and the insight Studio

The new For You section shows the insights the workspace features. The Studio is where AI-drafted insights are generated, and a configurable gate decides whether each one is featured automatically or waits for the workspace admin to approve or reject it.

Every number is checked

Each draft insight must pass two checks. Every number in it must be a metric computed by compute_metric from a query result, and every chart must read only this run's datasets and render headless. A draft that fails gets one repair turn, then it's dropped, and a shallow run lists what it threw out.

The workspace suggests what to read next

The ranking is deliberately simple and readable today. An insight's own score, plus 0.25 per topic it shares with what you're reading, 0.1 for the same source, 0.15 for the same view, and 0.1 if a person featured it. Insights you've opened this session drop behind ones you haven't. Dismissing one says "You'll see fewer insights like this," with an undo. Future work should make this work across sessions and users too.

05Chat, and promoting what it finds

A chat response, a query result, or an AI-generated insight all share one Save button. One click keeps it in your personal Library, and promoting it writes it into its package for everyone in the workspace.

Chat answerA draft; nothing kept yet
→
Save for meAn analysis in your personal Library
→
Promote: Save to storefront…A saved insight at public/apps/<slug>/
→
Also add its queryOptional: a view: or query: in the model

Promoting writes the answer into the package as the insight primitive proposed in Part 1, a manifest-backed data app: the claim, the details, the evidence chart, the queries, and a snapshot of the rows, written through PUT …/data-apps/{slug} with the same compile-first, hash-checked, rollback rules as the dashboard write. Once saved, it is the same object in both places. The Console lists it under the package, an agent finds it with get_context, and it's reviewed and versioned with the model. The toggle at the bottom continues into the promote wizard, which can also add the query to the model as a named view: or query:.

06Making it yours: themes and white-labeling

A workspace should feel like home. Borrowing from embedded analytics, that means it should look like its owner's product, not like ours.

Everything lives in one menu. Switch workspace; set the name, logo and packages; choose System, Light or Dark; pick the workspace theme; and choose who the analyst treats you as (an Admin sees the trace, an End user doesn't).

How theming works

  • Two independent axes. Mode (system, light or dark) is personal. The theme belongs to the workspace. Custom themes are shared across workspaces.
  • Every token is derived, not hand-picked. From one 11-step gray ramp and one 4-step accent, lib/theme.ts writes every shadcn token for both modes: background, card, muted, border, ring, primary, and the five sidebar tokens. Dark surfaces are made by lifting the theme's dark background toward a mid-tone of its own ramp with color-mix(in oklab, …), because ramps differ in how dark their low end is. So every utility class, including bg-chart-2/15, follows the theme with no per-component work.
  • Charts follow the accent. The five chart colors stay the same, but their order rotates so the series nearest the accent's hue comes first, keeping the adjacency the palette was designed around. Renderers that can't read oklch() (Vega) get hex.
  • Embedded content matches. useDataAppTheme() reads the computed tokens off the document and passes them to every embedded Publisher dashboard and data app as a DataAppTheme. The dashboard thumbnails in the white-label shot below are Publisher content, drawn in the workspace's colors.

07The composer: point at the object-level thing

Questions about data go wrong when the question is vague about what it's about. In the composer, you type @ and pull in the exact entity you want to reference.

Eight kinds of entity can be mentioned: sources, dashboards, notebooks, data apps, models, packages, and this app's own insights and threads. Handles are unique per workspace, prefixed with the package when two collide. A mention sends the entity's definition to the AI analyst as an attachment, and, more usefully, helps the AI analyst understand what the answer should be grounded in. The composer also takes uploaded attachments and transcribes voice input.

Credible One is an alias for Opus 5.5, the model Credible recommends today. Picking it means picking Credible's recommendation rather than a specific vendor's model: when a better model ships, Credible One moves to it, and nobody has to change a setting or retrain a habit. The other entries in the picker are there for teams that have a contract, a data-residency rule, or a preference that points at a particular provider.

Because every request already passes through it, Credible One is also where improvement can land later. It could become a model tuned for analytics work (writing Malloy, choosing the right grain, refusing to guess a field name), or one adapted to a single workspace, learning from its models, its saved insights, and the answers its analysts have marked correct. Either way, the name in the picker stays the same.

08Where it could go

Together, the prototype's parts make one loop: what the workspace values steers what the AI looks for, which content is featured, and what the AI finds eventually turns into proposed changes to the model. The pieces exist here in rough form, but none of it has been compared against the real product or validated by real users yet.

1 · Curate
What the workspace values Featured and dismissed insights, saves, follows, collections, comments, channels, and what people access and how frequently.
2 · Steer
Recipes that learn Those signals become the Studio's instructions, per person and per workspace, instead of a fixed daily recipe.
3 · Explore
Autonomous analysis Runs that follow up on their own findings: a change leads to a driver, then a segment, then a comparison against last year.
4 · Propose
Semantic exploration Walk the model's graph, then propose the dimension, measure, join or #(doc) that would have made a finding easier to reach.
↺ what people do with step 4's proposals feeds back into step 1

Some ideas from this prototype I'd like to explore further:

  • One content library for people and teams. The Library becomes the shared home for everything a user or team makes, not just what ships in a package: share an insight or query with a teammate or agent, comment and iterate on it together, and curate content for the team, whether or not it ever gets promoted into the package.
  • A first-party chat client. The composer goes well beyond the Credible App's workspace chat bar, and that may cut against how Credible pitches itself today: as the engine that plugs into the agent you already use (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, or Cursor and Claude Code for coding) over MCP, rather than another vendor's chat window. Owning the client is what makes @ mentions of workspace objects, the context budget, and one-click saving to the Library possible, but it competes with tools analysts already use. One middle path is to keep MCP as the primary surface and treat the composer as the in-product option for people who don't live in an AI client.
  • Insights that check themselves. A saved insight keeps the rows it was written against, so re-running it on a schedule and flagging when the live chart disagrees with the text description is cheap. The tight end cliff in Part 1 already records its date for this reason, and it's the natural place to meet Publisher's materialization scheduler.
  • Model proposals. Walk the model as a graph to find gaps (measures nothing reads, joins that fan out, fields with no #(doc), chat questions that missed) and propose edits with a receipt for every claim, the way eval-diagnose and eval-improve already do for eval failures.

All of this sits on platform work the prototype assumes already exists. Library state lives in localStorage instead of a store the host owns, there's no signed-in identity to carry user-attribute based givens and #(authorize) through, and the Studio's schedule isn't a real job. These are some of the pieces most likely to already have precedent in the Credible app.

09Next steps

The assignment was open-ended: play around with Publisher. I picked these directions by following what looked interesting, without seeing the Credible app, its users, or its roadmap. As a next step I'd like to find out which of these ideas matter most.

  1. Get access to the Credible web app. All of Part 2 was built without seeing it, and every step below depends on it.
  2. Audit what the platform already does. Map each prototype piece against the app: content browsing, chat and grounding, saved content, identity and permissions, persistence, scheduling, and theming. I'll mark each one as already solved, solved differently, or missing, and compare the prototype's concepts against the app's.
  3. Sync on the ideas. Chat with the team on anything from these documents worth exploring further. The ideas that change Publisher itself need the most scrutiny: Question mode, the Console Publish button, the app.json insight primitive, and the friction fixes from Part 1.
  4. Find the higher-priority work. Customer requests, known gaps, and roadmap commitments almost certainly outrank anything here. I'd be thrilled to see even just one or two of these ideas survive, folded into work that's already planned.
